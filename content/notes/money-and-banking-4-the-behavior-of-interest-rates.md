Let's examine how the overall level of nominal interest rates is determined and which factors influence their behavior. 

## Determinants of Asset Demand
Each statement below holds everything else constant. 

### Wealth
When wealth rises, people have more resources to buy assets, so the quantity demanded of an asset rises. 

### Expected Return
When an asset is expected to earn more than its alternatives, the quantity demanded of it rises. 

### Risk
When an asset becomes riskier than its alternatives, the quantity demanded of it falls. 

### Liquidity
When an asset is easier to sell quickly than its alternatives, the quantity demanded of it rises. 

| Variable ↑ | Quantity demanded |
|---|---|
| Wealth | ↑ |
| Expected return (relative) | ↑ |
| Risk (relative) | ↓ |
| Liquidity (relative) | ↑ |

## The Liquidity Preference Framework
The model states that the interest rate is set by the supply of and demand for money, not bonds. **People hold their wealth as either money or bonds**, so the two markets are tied together by the following equations.     

$$B^s + M^s = B^d + M^d$$

$$B^s - B^d = M^d - M^s \quad\Longrightarrow\quad M^d = M^s \iff B^d = B^s$$
